## Key Companies

### Bently Nevada (Baker Hughes)

#### Background and History
- **Founded:** 1956 in Berkeley, California (as Bently Scientific Company); incorporated in Minden, Nevada in 1961
- **Founder:** Donald E. Bently, who started with three employees
- **Major Acquisition:** Sold to GE Energy in January 2002 for between $600-900 million
- **Current Ownership:** Remained part of Baker Hughes after GE's sale of its interest in the company
- **Legacy:** Almost 70 years of innovation in asset management and machinery protection

#### Key Contributions to the Field
1. **Eddy Current Proximity Probe (1960s):** Don Bently was the first to manufacture a commercially successful eddy-current proximity probe, which measured vibration in high-speed turbomachinery by allowing direct observation of the rotating shaft
2. **Industry Standard Technology:** In 1970, the American Petroleum Institute designated the proximity probe as the measurement device for measuring acceptable shaft vibration during factory acceptance testing
3. **Pioneering Vibration Analysis:** Invented vibration analysis six decades ago, pioneering a new era in monitoring and protection of critical industrial assets
4. **Rotordynamics Research:** Advanced research in rotordynamics, furthering knowledge of machinery malfunctions such as shaft cracks and fluid-induced instabilities
5. **Standards Development:** Made key contributions to machine monitoring standards, including API 670

#### Notable Technologies and Products
- Eddy-current proximity transducers (non-contacting displacement sensors)
- 3500 Machinery Protection Systems (over 80,000 deployed globally)
- Over 6 million sensors installed worldwide
- More than 10,000 CMMS-based remote monitoring software solutions in use

#### Current Influence and Legacy
- The eddy-current proximity probe became the de facto standard for the turbomachinery industry
- Bently's application of eddy-current sensor technology was the foundation of an entire industry surrounding vibration instrumentation for machinery protection and diagnostics
- More end users trust their operational efficiency to Bently Nevada than anyone else in the field
- Continues to be the leading brand in machinery protection and condition monitoring

#### ASME Recognition
#282 Bently Nevada's Non-Contacting Eddy Current Sensors recognized as an ASME landmark

### F. Stanley Nowlan and Howard F. Heap

#### Background
- **Affiliation:** United Airlines
- **Sponsor:** U.S. Department of Defense (Manpower, Reserve Affairs and Logistics)
- **Publication Date:** December 29, 1978
- **Report Title:** "Reliability-Centered Maintenance"

#### Key Contributions
1. **Original RCM Methodology:** Developed foundational RCM process for aviation industry
2. **Paradigm Shift:** Showed that condition-based maintenance was more effective than age-based
3. **Scientific Foundation:** Research into failure and accident causes in aviation
4. **DOD Sponsorship:** Work performed under defense department contract

#### Notable Publications
- **"Reliability-Centered Maintenance" (1978):** Original textbook defining RCM
- **Rand Corporation Evaluation Report (1978):** Independent assessment of RCM approach

#### Term Attribution
The term "reliability-centered maintenance" was actually coined by Tom Matteson, Stanley Nowlan, and Howard Heap, though Matteson received no authorial credit having left United Airlines before final publication.

#### Current Influence and Legacy
- Foundation for all modern RCM approaches
- Original methodology still fundamentally unchanged since 1978
- Work remains required reading for RCM practitioners
- Influenced aviation maintenance worldwide and expanded to all industries

#### Relationships to Other Contributors
- Howard Heap passed away shortly after publication
- Stan Nowlan collaborated with John Moubray starting in 1983
- Work influenced Anthony M. Smith, Jack Nicholas, and entire RCM community
- Foundation for Aladon's RCM2 and later RCM3 developments

## Important Concepts and Tools

### The P-F Curve (Potential to Functional Failure)

#### Background and History
- **Introduced:** 1978 by F. Stanley Nowlan and Howard F. Heap (United Airlines)
- **Context:** Part of original RCM methodology
- **Paradigm:** Showed condition-based maintenance more effective than age-based

#### Key Definitions
- **Potential Failure (P):** Detectable state of failure; point where degradation begins
- **Functional Failure (F):** Asset or component has reached failed state or no longer performs satisfactorily
- **P-F Interval:** Time between potential failure detection and functional failure occurrence

#### Significance
- Visualization of asset condition and practical lifespan
- Determines inspection frequency and monitoring methods
- Guides condition monitoring technology selection
- Foundation for predictive maintenance programs

#### Relationship to Condition Monitoring
- More sensitive monitoring = longer P-F interval
- More frequent inspections = earlier P detection
- Different technologies detect at different P points
- Online monitoring provides longest P-F intervals

#### Current Applications
- RCM analysis and task selection
- Condition monitoring program design
- Maintenance interval determination
- Technology selection and justification
- Training and communication tool

---

### RCM (Reliability-Centered Maintenance)

#### Origins
- **1978:** Nowlan and Heap report for U.S. Department of Defense
- **Aviation Industry:** United Airlines research on commercial aviation
- **Military Application:** DOD-sponsored for military aircraft maintenance

#### Evolution
- **1983-1991:** John Moubray adapted for industry (RCM2)
- **1993:** Anthony M. Smith's industrial applications
- **2000s:** RCM3 development (Marius Basson, Aladon)

#### Seven Questions (RCM2 Process)
1. What are the functions and desired performance standards?
2. In what ways can it fail to fulfill its functions?
3. What causes each functional failure?
4. What happens when each failure occurs?
5. In what way does each failure matter?
6. What can be done to predict or prevent each failure?
7. What should be done if a suitable proactive task cannot be found?

#### Current Influence
- Foundational methodology for reliability programs
- Applied across all industries
- Basis for asset management strategies
- Integrated with ISO 55000 and other standards

### Swiss Cheese Model

#### Background
- **Developer:** Professor James T. Reason
- **Published:** 1991 in "Human Error"
- **Field:** Human factors and safety science

#### Concept
Multiple layers of defense (Swiss cheese slices) with random holes (weaknesses):
- Organizational influences
- Unsafe supervision
- Preconditions for unsafe acts
- Unsafe acts
- Defenses

Accident occurs when holes align across all layers.

#### Applications
- Aviation safety
- Healthcare (patient safety)
- Engineering
- Emergency services
- Cybersecurity (defense in depth)
- Power generation
- Construction
- Maintenance and reliability

#### Maintenance and Reliability Applications
- Maintenance error reduction
- Procedure development
- Training program design
- Incident investigation
- Barrier analysis
- Safety culture development

#### Current Influence
- Essential tool for safety professionals globally
- Evolved into Safety-II and resilience engineering concepts
- Applied to maintenance-induced failures
- Integrated into reliability and maintenance programs

### Weibull Analysis

#### Background
- **Named After:** Waloddi Weibull (Swedish engineer)
- **Modern Methods:** Dr. Robert B. Abernethy and others
- **Applications:** Lifetime data analysis, failure prediction

#### Key Capabilities
- Works with extremely small samples (2-3 failures)
- Applicable to 85-95% of all life data
- Predicts failure probability over time
- Determines maintenance intervals
- Supports warranty analysis
- Enables life cycle cost analysis

#### Parameters
- **Beta (β):** Shape parameter indicating failure mode
  - β < 1: Infant mortality (decreasing failure rate)
  - β = 1: Random failures (constant failure rate)
  - β > 1: Wear out (increasing failure rate)
- **Eta (η):** Scale parameter (characteristic life)
- **Gamma (γ):** Location parameter (failure-free period)

#### Applications in Reliability
- Failure mode analysis
- Spare parts optimization
- Warranty cost prediction
- Design improvement
- Accelerated life testing
- Reliability demonstration

#### Current Influence
- Standard tool for reliability engineers
- Integrated into reliability software
- Required for aerospace and defense
- Applied across all industries

### Motor Current Signature Analysis (MCSA)

#### Background
- **Origins:** Early 1970s for nuclear power plants
- **Purpose:** Monitor motors in hazardous or harsh environments
- **Technology:** Analysis of electrical current and voltage signatures

#### Capabilities
- **Non-intrusive:** No sensor installation on motor
- **Online Monitoring:** No production interruption required
- **Electrical and Mechanical Faults:** Distinguishes fault types
- **Early Detection:** Identifies problems in initial stages

#### Faults Detected
- Air-gap eccentricity (non-uniform air gap)
- Broken rotor bars
- Bearing faults
- Misalignment
- Load variations
- Electrical imbalances

#### Advantages
- Simpler sensor installation (in MCC cabinet)
- Reduced sensitivity to installation location
- Less sensitive to background noise
- Cost-effective compared to vibration monitoring
- Covers electrical and mechanical issues

#### Current Applications
- Critical motor monitoring
- Remote or hazardous location motors
- Complement to vibration analysis
- Integration with motor control systems

#### Technologies

#### Applications
- Wear metal trending

- Lubricant degradation tracking
- Filter effectiveness

#### Current Influence
- Integral to reliability programs
- Complement to vibration and thermography
